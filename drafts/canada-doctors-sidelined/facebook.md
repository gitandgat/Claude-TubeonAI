# Facebook Post — Canada Doctors Sidelined

Can I ask you something?

If you trained for years to become a doctor or a nurse — cleared every exam, built every clinical skill, gave a decade of your life to it — and then a licensing body told you that your credentials didn't count here...

What would you do next?

I've been thinking about this a lot. Canada has 20,000 immigrant physicians and 32,000 immigrant nurses who are fully qualified but can't practice medicine because of licensing barriers. Not incompetence. Not safety concerns. Paperwork and gatekeeping from a system that wasn't built for them.

It's a headline that should make us angry. But it also holds a mirror up to something I see every day in the healthcare professionals I work with.

The system was never designed to put your needs first.

The licensing board doesn't see your ten-year career. It sees a checklist. The hospital administration doesn't see your burnout. It sees a staffing number. The credentialing committee doesn't see the human being who has given everything to this work — it sees a form that doesn't quite fit.

And when the system keeps not seeing you, it's very easy to start believing the system is right.

It isn't.

If you're a healthcare professional who has ever felt invisible inside the very institution you gave everything to — I want you to know that the crosswalk is real. There is another side to reach. And the path there starts with refusing to let the system define your worth.
