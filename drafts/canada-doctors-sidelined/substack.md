# Substack Newsletter — Canada Doctors Sidelined

SUBJECT LINE: The system that keeps 52,000 healthcare workers out
PREVIEW TEXT: Credentialing barriers. And what they reveal about how the system sees us.

---

This week, a statistic stopped me mid-sentence.

Canada has 20,000 trained immigrant physicians and 32,000 immigrant nurses living in the country right now — fully qualified, ready to work — who are not allowed to practice medicine. Not because they're unqualified. Because of licensing barriers. Credentialing bureaucracy. A system that moves slower than the need it's supposed to serve.

The number itself is staggering. But what stayed with me isn't the policy failure. It's what the policy failure reveals.

**The Lesson**

The system was not designed to see you.

I don't mean that as cynicism. I mean it as an accurate description of how institutions work. A licensing body sees a checklist. A hospital administration sees a staffing grid. A credentialing committee sees a form — and if your form doesn't match the template, the system produces friction, not flexibility.

Healthcare professionals know this from the inside. You've navigated credentialing committees, documentation requirements, scope-of-practice restrictions that seem designed to protect the institution rather than the patient. You've felt the gap between what you know how to do and what you're officially allowed to do.

And when the system keeps producing friction — when the bureaucracy keeps saying "not yet, not here, not this way" — it takes something from you. Not just time. Something quieter. The sense that maybe the system is right. That maybe there's something missing.

It doesn't mean that.

Fifty-two thousand trained healthcare professionals are on the sidelines not because they lack ability, but because institutions move slowly and gatekeepers protect gates.

The crosswalk lesson here is this: when the system fails to see your value, it is telling you something about the system. It is not telling you something about your value.

**One Question**

Where in your career have you let the system's verdict become your verdict?

**This Week's Practice**

Write down one qualification, capability, or contribution that your current institution has never formally acknowledged. Keep it somewhere you'll see it this week.
