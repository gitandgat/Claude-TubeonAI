[Slide 1]
You don't have a laziness problem.
You have an avoidance loop.

[Slide 2]
Procrastination is not a time problem.
Researcher Tim Pychyl found it's an emotion problem. When a task feels threatening, your brain triggers dread — and you escape it. That escape feels like relief. Relief becomes a reward. The loop repeats.

[Slide 3]
Your amygdala treats "career change" like a predator.
It fires the same alarm for a difficult email that it fires for physical danger. You close the tab. You feel better. Your brain files that as: avoidance works. Do it again next time.

[Slide 4]
The longer you avoid, the more automatic it gets.
Every avoided step strengthens the procrastination pathway. Every step not taken weakens the action pathway. That's not character — it's neurobiology.

[Slide 5]
You're not avoiding the task.
You're avoiding how you expect the task will make you feel. And Pychyl's research found that almost every time, people were wrong about how bad it would be.

[Slide 6]
The fix is embarrassingly small.
Name the emotion ("I'm overwhelmed / afraid / not sure I'm qualified"). Shrink the action to something the amygdala won't flag as a threat. Start for five minutes. That's it. That interrupts the loop.
