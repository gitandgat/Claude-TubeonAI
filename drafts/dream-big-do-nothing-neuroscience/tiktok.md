[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Your brain is running a protection loop."]

When you think about doing the scary thing — sending the application, making the call, starting the pivot — your amygdala fires. That's your threat detector. And it does not know the difference between "bear chasing me" and "opening a scary email."

[VISUAL: Quick animation of brain lighting up]

[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Dread → Avoidance → Relief → Repeat"]

You feel dread. You avoid. You feel relief. Your brain logs that as: this worked. Do it again. Every time you repeat this loop, the avoidance gets more automatic.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: "Researcher Tim Pychyl called it an emotion regulation problem."]

You're not avoiding the task. You're avoiding how you expect the task will make you feel.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: "And you're almost always wrong about how bad it will be."]

His research: people who finally started said the task was easier than expected — and they all wished they'd started sooner.

[ON SCREEN TEXT: "The fix: 3 steps."]

One — name the emotion. Say it out loud: "I'm anxious. I'm afraid I'll fail."
Two — shrink the task so small your amygdala doesn't flag it. Not "research a new career." Just open one job posting and read it.
Three — start. Five minutes. That's the loop-breaker.
