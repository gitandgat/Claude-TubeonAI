Courage isn't a personality trait. It's a neural circuit — and Stanford found the exact switch.

Researchers identified a cluster of neurons that fires when people act despite fear. It's measurable, and it's trainable. The physiological sigh — a double inhale through your nose followed by a long exhale — reduces your fear response in real time. No years of therapy required. Two breaths.

The research doesn't say wait until you feel ready. It says act while afraid, and the circuit strengthens every time.
