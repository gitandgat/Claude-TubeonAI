Courage isn't a personality trait. It's a neural circuit — and it can be trained.

Stanford researchers identified a specific neural circuit they call the "courage switch" — a cluster of neurons in the subgenual anterior cingulate cortex that activates when people act despite fear. This isn't metaphor. It's measurable.

The implication: courage is not something you have or don't have. It's something you practice.

And there's a fast entry point. The physiological sigh — a double inhale through the nose followed by a long exhale — reduces sympathetic activation in real time. Tested, measured, reproducible. You can use it before a hard conversation, before submitting a resignation letter, before doing anything that scares you.

**You can't think your way into courage. You can only act your way into it.**

The research doesn't say wait until you're ready. It says act while afraid — and the circuit strengthens.
