# LinkedIn — canada-doctors-sidelined

52,000 qualified healthcare professionals in Canada. None of them allowed to practice.

Not because they failed a licensing exam.
Not because there's no need.
Because a credentialing system moves slower than the crisis it was designed to prevent.

Canada has 20,000 immigrant physicians and 32,000 immigrant nurses — fully trained, living here — who cannot see a single patient. Meanwhile, emergency rooms close for lack of staff and healthcare workers inside the system burn out at record rates.

The gap between those two facts is not a coincidence. It's a systems failure.

Here's what I think it reveals:

Institutions see credentials, not people. A licensing board processes applications. A credentialing committee reviews forms. None of those functions, by design, can see the clinical judgment you've built across thousands of patient encounters. The depth that doesn't fit on a form.

And when the system can't see something, it treats it as if it doesn't exist.

For the 52,000 outside the gate, the consequence is visible. For healthcare professionals inside the system — navigating scope-of-practice restrictions, administrative burden, roles that don't match their capability — the same dynamic plays out more quietly.

The institution doesn't see you. After a while, it's easy to stop seeing yourself.

That's the part worth naming.
