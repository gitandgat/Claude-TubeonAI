Something I hear constantly from nurses, doctors, and PAs who know they need to make a change: "I'm just not ready yet."

And I get it. I said the same thing for longer than I want to admit.

But here's the thing — your nervous system isn't scanning for whether you're ready. It's scanning for whether this is familiar. Anything outside your current self-concept gets flagged as threat, even when it's exactly the right move. That's not a warning. That's identity stretch. And it happens before every meaningful transition.

Your brain doesn't update through encouragement or positive self-talk. It updates through proof. Small, adjacent proof. Say one sentence instead of staying silent. Make the decision without waiting for certainty. Post the draft before it's perfect. Each action sends a signal to your nervous system that says: we've done something like this before, and nothing collapsed.

The 24-hour rule changed how I move: when self-doubt shows up, take one next visible step within 24 hours. Not the whole plan — just the next move. Because self-doubt is manageable when it's fresh. Leave it for a week and it starts to feel like a character trait rather than a passing signal.

The goal was never to eliminate self-doubt. It's to outgrow it.
