Have you ever caught yourself describing the life you want in perfect detail — and then doing nothing about it for months?

I used to think that was a character flaw. The neuroscience says something different. When you think about a high-stakes next step, your brain's threat detector fires. You feel dread. You avoid. You feel relief. And that relief becomes a reward — one your brain wants to repeat every time the uncomfortable task comes up. Researcher Tim Pychyl called it an emotion regulation problem. The deeper finding: you're not avoiding the task. You're avoiding how you expect the task will make you feel.

And you're almost always wrong about how bad it will actually be.
