# Facebook Caption

Something I keep seeing in the people I talk to:

They're not failing at recovery. They're succeeding at the wrong habits — the ones that made sense in survival mode and never got turned off.

Saying yes to everything before checking what they have left. Going to bed late as a form of revenge — scrolling to reclaim "me time" that costs more than it gives. Holding pre-burnout standards against a post-burnout body and feeling like a failure when they don't measure up.

The one that delays recovery the most, though? Managing it alone.

High achievers are very good at looking fine. Too good. If you don't tell someone what's actually happening — not the managed version, the real one — no one can help you stop.

Your body sent the signal long before your brain was ready to hear it.

Recovery is slow. It starts with one stopped habit.
