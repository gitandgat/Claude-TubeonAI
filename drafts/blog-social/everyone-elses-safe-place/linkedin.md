# LinkedIn — everyone-elses-safe-place
# Scheduled: 2026-04-09T15:00:00 ET

The most reliable person in the room is usually the loneliest one in it.

We check in on people who look like they're struggling. We leave alone the person holding everyone else together — because they look fine.

That gap between what you project and what you're actually carrying is the problem.

There's a word for the stress response that makes you accommodate, appease, and manage everyone else's distress before your own: fawning. It's not a personality trait. It's an adaptation — often from early environments where being calm and reliable kept things stable.

For healthcare professionals, it compounds fast. You absorb patients' fear, families' grief, colleagues' stress at work. Then go home and do it again.

Your nervous system doesn't distinguish between hour ten of a brutal shift and the third conversation today where someone else's needs came first. Masked activation is still activation. The calm you project isn't immunity — it's a practiced response.

One shift that helps: create space between the request and your answer. "Let me check and get back to you" interrupts the automatic yes. That pause is where you get to actually assess capacity — instead of agreeing out of habit, guilt, or a lifetime of being the person who always shows up.

Support that requires your self-erasure doesn't help anyone long-term. Not them. Not you.
