I've been thinking about the people who are always fine.

The ones who show up, hold space, answer the calls, and never seem to need anything themselves. Healthcare professionals are especially prone to this — emotional labor is literally part of the job description, and then it follows you home.

There's a stress response called fawning that turns caretaking into an automatic reflex. The harder you've worked to be reliable, the more invisible your own exhaustion becomes. And the more composed you look, the less anyone thinks to check on you.

That's not a flaw in the people around you. It's what happens when one person becomes the structural support for everyone else.
