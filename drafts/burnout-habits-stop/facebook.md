# Facebook Post

Can I ask you something honest?

When did you last leave work — and actually leave?

Not bring it home in your body. Not replay the difficult patient on the drive back. Not wake up at 3am still solving a problem from yesterday's shift.

For a lot of healthcare workers, the body clocks out. But everything else stays behind.

That's not a character flaw. It's what happens when you've trained yourself to push through everything, hold everything in your head, say yes to everything — for years on end.

Your body didn't break down because you're weak. It broke down because you were very, very good at overriding it.

Here's what I've noticed actually helps — not the wellness advice you've already heard, but the specific habits worth stopping:

Stop pretending you can manage this alone. High achievers look fine on the outside. That fools everyone — including yourself. If you don't name what's happening, recovery takes longer. Tell someone. See your GP.

Stop holding yourself to pre-burnout standards. The version of you who could work out five times a week and cook dinner and be fully present — that version needed rest too. Give yourself a scaled version of everything right now. Progress doesn't have to look impressive.

Stop living in constant urgency. Rushing through meals, chores, conversations — that's not efficiency. That's a nervous system that never got the signal it was safe to slow down. Practice asking: do I actually need to rush right now?

Burnout recovery isn't an event. It's a slow accumulation of stopped habits.

One at a time.
