# Substack Newsletter

SUBJECT LINE: Your body knew first

PREVIEW TEXT: The 10 habits keeping burnout in place — and the one that matters most.

---

This week, I've been thinking about what it means to override yourself.

Not once. Not when it counts. But as a lifestyle. As something you became excellent at.

Healthcare trains you for this. Push through the shift. Smile through the hard conversation. Hold it together in front of the patient. Clock out, drive home, and do it again tomorrow.

The problem isn't that you did that. The problem is that your body never got the signal it was allowed to stop.

---

THE LESSON

Burnout isn't a single moment. It's a slow accumulation of habits that made sense once — and stopped making sense a long time ago.

Ten of them keep showing up again and again in the people I talk to.

Saying yes before checking if you have anything left to give. Going to bed late as a way of stealing back "me time" — and calling it rest when it isn't. Holding yourself to the standards of a version of you that was running on a different tank. Trying to manage all of it alone.

That last one is the one I want to sit with.

High achievers are very good at looking fine. That competence — that ability to function through almost anything — becomes its own trap. If you look okay, people assume you are okay. You might assume it too.

But here is what I've learned from my own crossing: the body knew before the brain did. The fatigue. The numbness. The crying in the parking lot before a shift. That wasn't weakness. That was your nervous system sending a signal you'd learned to ignore.

The recovery isn't complicated. But it is slow. And it starts with one thing: letting someone else in on what's actually happening.

Not performing wellness. Not fixing it privately. Not adding one more self-care practice to an already exhausted schedule.

Just — telling the truth to someone who can help.

---

ONE QUESTION

What would you say if someone asked how you were really doing?

THIS WEEK'S PRACTICE

This week, write down one thing you've been carrying alone — and tell one person about it.
