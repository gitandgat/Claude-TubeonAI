Your body quit before you did.

These 10 habits are why it couldn't come back.

Most burned-out healthcare professionals aren't failing at self-care.

They're succeeding at the wrong habits — the ones that keep the nervous system stuck in fight-or-flight long after the shift ends.

Here's what to actually stop:

1. Saying yes to everything (check your time, energy, attention, resources — not just your calendar)
2. Going to bed late as revenge ("doom-scrolling" to steal back "me time" costs more than it gives)
3. Holding yourself to pre-burnout standards (traffic light system: Green = full / Amber = scaled / Red = 2 minutes of stretching counts)
4. Managing it alone (high achievers look fine on the outside — that's the trap)
5. Talking about work constantly (venting keeps your brain at the hospital even when your body left)
6. Pushing through everything (you don't have willpower reserves — you have a nervous system that's been overridden)
7. Living at high speed (rushing everywhere is a symptom, not a personality)
8. Holding everything in your head (stress degrades memory — write it down, let your brain rest)
9. Surviving on caffeine and convenience food (your body is trying to rebuild — give it something to work with)
10. Skipping food, water, and bathroom breaks at work (basic physiology isn't optional, even at your hospital)

Burnout recovery doesn't happen in a retreat weekend.

It happens in the slow accumulation of stopped habits.

Which one on this list do you need to hear most today?
