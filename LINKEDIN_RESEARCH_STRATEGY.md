# LinkedIn Research Strategy
## How to Position Yourself & Get Agency Owners to Talk

---

## THE STRATEGY (In Plain English)

You're NOT selling yet. You're researching.

Your LinkedIn should:
1. Show you understand agency problems (credibility)
2. Ask questions that get agency owners talking (research data)
3. Build your expert positioning (foundation for later sales)

Think of this as a **research campaign**, not a sales funnel.

---

## PART 1: LINKEDIN CONTENT CALENDAR
### 7-Post Series (Post 1-2x per week for 4 weeks)

---

### POST 1: The Problem (Day 1)
**Headline:** "I talked to 5 agency owners this week. Same complaint every time."

**Body:**
```
"How much time does your agency waste on things that don't make money?"

I'm asking because I just finished calls with 5 agency owners, and every single one said the same thing:

- Proposals take 4+ hours each
- Client reports are a Friday afternoon nightmare
- Invoicing & follow-ups fall through the cracks
- Content briefs feel like busywork

One owner told me: "I'm billing clients $5K/month for strategy, but I'm spending 15 hours a week on admin instead of being strategy."

Here's what surprised me: None of them were looking for more tools. They were looking for someone who understands their world.

My question for you: What's eating your time?

#Agency #OperationsEfficiency
```

**Why it works:**
- Shows empathy for their problem
- Asks a real question (gets comments)
- Makes YOU look like you're researching (not selling)

## PART 2: INTERVIEW QUESTIONS
### Use These in DMs & Calls

---

### Opening (Build Rapport)
```
"Hey [Name]—thanks for being open to this. I'm researching how agencies actually operate, 
and I'd love to understand your world better. Would it help if I walked you through a few 
questions, or would you rather just talk about what's on your mind?"
```

---

### Core Questions

**QUESTION 1: Identify Pain Point**
```
"What's eating the most time in your agency right now? 
Not client work—I mean the operational stuff that doesn't generate revenue."
```

*Why:* Opens up about real bottleneck. Listen for: proposals, reports, admin, content.

---

**QUESTION 2: AI Awareness**
```
"Have you tried using AI tools in your agency? 
If yes, what worked and what didn't? 
If no, what's held you back?"
```

*Why:* Understand where they are in the AI journey. Most will say "tried but didn't work."

---

**QUESTION 3: The Gap**
```
"If I said there's a way to cut that [biggest bottleneck] in half, 
would that be something you'd be interested in?"
```

*Why:* Gauge buying intent. If they say "maybe" or "yes" = qualified.

---

**QUESTION 4: Financial Validation**
```
"How many hours per week do you think you're losing to [that bottleneck]?"
```

*Why:* Get specific number. Then you calculate $ impact in your head.

If they say "10 hours/week" and they bill at $150/hr, that's $1,500/week or $6,000/month. Easy.

---

**QUESTION 5: Decision-Making**
```
"If you wanted to fix this, who would make the decision? You, your operations manager, 
someone else?"
```

*Why:* Understand the buying committee. Critical for later sales.

---

**QUESTION 6: The Trust Question**
```
"What would need to be true for you to hire someone to help you with this? 
Like, what would they need to prove to you?"
```

*Why:* Understand objections. This is how you position later.

Common answers:
- "They'd need to understand agencies" (you do—you researched)
- "They'd need to show ROI" (you have numbers)
- "They'd need references" (you'll get them from earlier clients)

---

**QUESTION 7: Closing**
```
"Based on what you told me, would this be something worth exploring further, 
or does it feel like it's not a priority right now?"
```

*Why:* Qualify them. Ends the call with clarity.

## PART 5: WHAT TO LISTEN FOR

As you do interviews, track:

### Pain Points (what they mention most)
- Proposals/contracts
- Client reporting
- Lead generation
- Team onboarding
- Email management
- CRM data entry

### Budget Reality
- "$5K would be worth it if it saved us time"
- "We can't afford another tool"
- "If it paid for itself in a month, sure"

### Their AI Maturity
- "We don't use AI at all"
- "We use ChatGPT for random stuff"
- "We tried to implement it but gave up"
- "We have a workflow that works"

### Decision-Making
- "I make the calls" = easy sale
- "I'd need to convince my partner" = harder sale
- "CFO controls spending" = know the real buyer

### What They Actually Want
- Help figuring out where to start (not tools)
- Someone who understands their business (not a tech consultant)
- Proof it works (case studies, ROI)
- Ongoing support (not a one-time thing)

---

## PART 6: AFTER YOUR INTERVIEWS

Once you've done 5-10 interviews, you'll know:
1. **Actual pain points** (not assumed)
2. **How much they'd pay** (budget reality)
3. **Who decides** (decision-makers)
4. **Why they'd hire you** (positioning)

Then you build:
- Your service offering (based on what they said, not what you think)
- Your positioning (based on what resonates)
- Your pricing (based on their budget)
- Your case study (from early conversations)

This is the opposite of the fantasy autonomous system. This is real market validation.
