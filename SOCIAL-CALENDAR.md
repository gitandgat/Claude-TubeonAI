**LinkedIn** [Pillar 2 — Psychology Framework]
- Format: Text post
- Post:
Alfred Adler believed that our deepest suffering comes not from what happens to us — but from the gap between who we are and who we feel we should be.

He called it the "inferiority gap." And I think it explains healthcare burnout better than any modern framework I've seen.

Think about it:

You entered healthcare with an ideal self — compassionate, competent, making a difference. That was the "should be."

Then reality happened. The system happened. The bureaucracy, the understaffing, the moral injury of knowing what your patients need and being unable to provide it.

And slowly, a gap opened. Between the healer you wanted to be and the worker you were forced to become.

That gap? That's where burnout lives. Not in the long hours. Not in the difficult patients. In the distance between your values and your daily reality.

Adler's solution wasn't to close the gap by trying harder. It was to redefine the ideal. To ask: "What if who I should be isn't who I was told to be — but who I actually am?"

That's the crossing. That's the work.

Not fixing yourself. Redefining what "fixed" even means.

If you're feeling that gap right now — between who you are at work and who you know yourself to be — it's not burnout telling you to rest. It's wisdom telling you to move.

- Healthcare adaptation: Make each slide specific to healthcare moments — "I'd tell her that the patient she lost on night shift wasn't her fault." "I'd tell her that crying in the break room doesn't make her weak."

**Format 3: "POV: You're a [Healthcare Worker] Who Just..." Reel**
- Platform: Instagram Reel
- Concept: First-person POV format. "POV: You're a nurse who just realized you've been crying in the parking lot every day this week and calling it 'fine.'" Pair with relatable audio (trending sound or a voiceover).
- Why it works: POV content creates instant identification. The viewer IS the character. It stops scrolls because it feels personally targeted.
- Healthcare adaptation: Draw from real scenarios — the parking lot, the charting at midnight, the "I'm fine" mask. Keep it emotionally honest, never mocking.

**Format 4: "Myth vs. Reality" Carousel**
- Platform: Instagram Carousel + LinkedIn
- Concept: Side-by-side debunking. "Myth: Leaving healthcare means you wasted your education." / "Reality: You graduated from healthcare. Your education is the foundation of everything you build next."
- Why it works: Contrarian content drives engagement because it challenges assumptions. People save it to reference later and share it to validate their own feelings.
- Healthcare adaptation: Myths about burnout, about leaving, about identity, about money. Endless content possibilities. Pull myths directly from community comments and DMs.

**Format 5: "Interview Yourself" Reel Series**
- Platform: Instagram Reel
- Concept: Sahawat asks himself questions on camera (split screen or alternating angles). "Why did you really leave medicine?" "What do you miss?" "What surprised you about the other side?" "What would you say to someone standing at the curb right now?"
- Why it works: Conversational, authentic, and creates an intimate feeling. The "interview" format gives permission to go deeper than a standard talking-head video.
- Healthcare adaptation: Make the questions the questions the audience is asking in DMs. This format lets you address real concerns in a personal, non-preachy way.
