Hi {{person.firstName}},

Through the 12-hour shift. Through the grief. Through the moral injury. Through the moment a patient dies and you have to walk into the next room and do it all again.

Pushing through is a skill. And it probably saved you — professionally and personally — more than once.

But in the ELDER stage, it stops working. Not because you've become weak. Because the load has exceeded the capacity to push.

**You can't push through depletion at the identity level. You can only move through it — slowly, with help.**

This is the hardest transition for high-functioning healthcare professionals to make: moving from "I can handle this myself" to "I need to receive something from someone else."

That shift — from doing to receiving — is the core work of the ELDER stage.

It requires trust. And trust requires vulnerability. Which is perhaps the thing the healthcare system trains you hardest to suppress.

So today's question is: **Who do you trust enough to be honest with about where you actually are?**

If the answer is "no one" — that's worth knowing. Building one honest relationship is the first act of healing in the ELDER stage.
